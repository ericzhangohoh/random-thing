## Task response and architecture

The official 2017 A prompt requires drone counts and initial coordinates for a Ferris wheel, dragon and original design; mathematical flight paths that animate them; show-wide resource and safety discussion; and a two-page mayoral memo. It explicitly does not require a working animation program. This paper supplies a substantial simulation implementation in addition to those requirements.

Its architecture is:

`Image/shape specification → sampled points → rigid-body or articulated-body coordinate model → time-dependent motion → point matching and transitions → speed/acceleration-based timing → shared fleet, space, schedule and budget → memo.`

The main decomposition is by **motion structure**, not by three unrelated images. Circular/rigid transformations support the Ferris wheel and clock; skeleton-linked transformations support the dragon; image-boundary sampling supports irregular shapes; a reusable transition algorithm connects the scenes (pp.5–9, 18–24). This creates a strong model-reuse narrative.

## Approach and justification

The authors distinguish regular rigid motion from a non-rigid articulated figure and explain why the latter needs a skeleton plus attached outline points (pp.5–7). They derive coordinates for the Ferris-wheel frame, spokes and two cabin arrangements, then extend the basic circular motion with arcuate spokes and expanding/contracting rims (pp.9–17). A planar link system models dragon joints; rotation matrices move associated contour points; a three-dimensional extension rotates the wings (pp.18–21).

The clock reuses earlier components instead of introducing another independent theory. Transition planning matches initial and target point sets and schedules departures to coordinate arrival (pp.21–24). The paper offers a cosine-law argument for increasing separation in a special radial-motion case, then develops a practical greedy matching approximation and a collision-related correction (pp.22–24).

**Reviewer inference:** the transferable strength is choosing a small set of mathematical representations that explain several required outputs. Each extension has an identifiable purpose: visual detail, articulated movement, dimensional extension or scene transition. The derivations are substantially more useful than naming transformation matrices without defining their geometric meaning. However, the special separation argument must retain its assumptions when applied to finite source patterns and unequal departure times.

## Data, assumptions and design parameters

The design uses a cited drone's size, speed, flight-time and altitude figures, then chooses a lower design speed, a spacing envelope and a shorter show duration (pp.25–27). The equipment and historical regulatory figures are reported source values from 2017, not current operating guidance. Assumptions ignore wind, treat drones as points and prescribe simplified acceleration/deceleration (p.7). The later timing formula distinguishes reaching cruising speed from shorter triangular-speed-profile flights (p.27).

Tables connect geometric parameters to display size, count and spacing constraints (pp.26–30). The show-level schedule then combines launch, transformation and display segments (p.31). This is a good example of taking local model outputs back into a system-wide resource check.

## Validation and limits of the claims

The paper presents numerous simulated positions and animation sequences, and includes program listings (pp.28–31, 34–41). These make the geometric intent inspectable. They do not independently establish all-pair clearance throughout continuous time, maximum acceleration at corners, communication/control feasibility, performance under wind, or a globally minimum transition time.

The transition section is especially instructive. The pairwise radial argument assumes a common center and simultaneous equal-speed radial motion (p.22); p.23 approximates a finite dense cloud by that center and introduces departure delays. P.24 itself gives a collision example and says a correction reduces collision frequency. A later claim of collision prevention is therefore stronger than the supplied evidence. The future-work section proposes adding collision detection (p.32). A rigorous workflow would require an explicit clearance test over every scene and transition, plus examination between sampled frames, rather than infer safety from attractive animations.

No baseline assignment comparison or formal optimality result is given for the matching heuristic. Runtime complexity and nearly unchanged results are asserted without a displayed benchmark suite (pp.23–24). These should be recorded as author claims awaiting independent checks.

## Static consistency checks

The abstract uses 528 drones, whereas the main design uses 420 plus 10 reserves (pp.1, 26, 31). A 20×22 grid has 440 cells, which needs distinguishing from the 430-drone inventory and quoted 430 square-meter area (pp.4, 26). The memo's rental total includes reserves while the later equipment total corresponds to active drones only (pp.4, 26, 31). These are reconcilable definitions only if made explicit.

The printed departure-delay expression on p.24 subtracts the first/farthest distance from the later distance, giving the opposite sign from the stated delay rationale; the appendix uses first distance minus later distance (p.40). Parameters in the Ferris-wheel appendix also differ from some parameter-table entries (pp.26–27, 34). These are concrete reasons to bind formulas, code configuration and reported results to the same design version.

## Transfer to the working document

1. Classify the mathematical structure of required behaviors before assigning one model per subquestion.
2. Reuse a justified representation where it fits, and state precisely what changes in an extension.
3. Separate shape recognition, geometric movement, physical trajectory feasibility and operational implementation; success at one level does not prove the next.
4. Require every proof-based argument to list its assumptions and check them after approximation or heuristic modification.
5. Treat simulations as measurements: define the safety/feasibility metric, temporal resolution and worst-case checks, not only rendered frames.
6. Preserve one fleet, space and timing ledger so local designs reconcile with the final memo.

The best lesson is the coherent progression from component geometry to scene transformations and then show-wide logistics. The additional validation work illustrates where an independent reviewer should challenge a claim without discarding the useful architecture.
