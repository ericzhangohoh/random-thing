## Task coverage and architecture

Against the official 2018 A prompt, the report supplies a specification-based rating, a top ten, two online comparisons, an app concept and a news release. Its distinctive decomposition is a benefit-minus-discomfort utility model:

`Data audit/correlation → mean or regression imputation → Box–Cox transformation and normal-CDF scaling → five-component thrill index → two logistic discomfort penalties → comprehensive score → survey/default weights or user AHP weights → ranked output and app.`

The paper develops the model incrementally. Height represents weightlessness, then speed and G-force are added; inversion count is replaced by inversion frequency; a type score completes the thrill index (pp.10–14). Discomfort responds nonlinearly to G-force and inversion frequency (pp.14–17). A weighted combination produces the published ranking (pp.25–28). This is a coherent explanation structure even where the particular proxies need better evidence.

## Author rationale versus reviewer inference

The authors explain why thrill alone is inadequate: an extreme ride can be exciting yet unpleasant. Conversely, minimizing discomfort alone can recommend an uninteresting ride. This motivates separate positive and negative components before combining them (pp.17–18). The same factor can therefore have both a benefit and a penalty. That is more expressive than declaring every specification universally beneficial.

Height is retained over drop because the two correlate strongly and height has fewer missing observations (pp.10–11). Inversion frequency distinguishes equal inversion counts over different ride durations (p.12). The claimed discomfort thresholds motivate sigmoid curves rather than linear penalties (pp.14–16). Relative user judgments motivate the criteria-weight portion of AHP; the existing numerical scores replace a second set of alternative comparisons (pp.20–25). Different levels of customization are matched to beginner, experienced and enthusiast users (p.19).

**Reviewer inference:** the strongest transferable lesson is to derive a baseline, identify a specific deficiency, then add the smallest mechanism that addresses it. The paper's physical explanation does not, by itself, validate height as actual weightlessness duration or scalar maximum G-force as a full motion/comfort exposure. Its claim that the selected variables are statistically independent is also inconsistent with its own height–speed correlation near 0.82 (pp.7, 10). A weighted sum can still be used, but independence and objectivity should not be asserted without support.

## Data, scaling and preference provenance

The missingness table is unusually useful: 53% missing drop, 25.67% duration, 72.67% G-force and 69.67% vertical angle (p.8). Regression relationships motivate imputation, but no held-out error estimates or imputation uncertainty are supplied. In particular, imputing one heavily missing field from another compounds uncertainty.

Box–Cox parameters and distribution diagrams make scaling visible (p.9). However, the transformation requires a documented policy for zero inversions, and transforming ratios must follow computation of the physical ratio. The same symbols are used for physical and normalized values, so interfaces should state clearly whether a threshold or division uses original units. The model's definitions of physiology are treated here as author modeling claims, not medical guidance.

A 46-response forum sample provides Borda scores for coaster type (pp.13–14). An additional online pairwise survey determines default weights (pp.26–27; complete marginal tables pp.39–49). These are subjective preference inputs, whereas the task's generic ranking is supposed to depend only on numerical/descriptive specifications. They fit the personalized-app extension more naturally than an unqualified objective default. The forum's selection bias, collapsed type categories and mapping of Floorless to Suspended need justification beyond a statement of transitive preferences.

The discomfort curves extrapolate population response from cited sources to coaster rides without actual ride-level calibration (pp.14–16, 29–30). A sum of two discomfort probabilities is an additive penalty up to two, not the probability of experiencing either symptom. The paper calls the sum an index, which should be preserved when communicating it.

## What is validated and what remains unsupported

Two external rankings are displayed, and the authors distinguish a crowd-vote site from one enthusiast's list (p.28). This helps explain why rankings answer different preference questions. It does not validate exact component weights or establish superiority. The report contains no displayed perturbation of logistic thresholds, imputed values, survey sampling or weights; the close top scores make such rank-stability checks especially relevant (p.27).

AHP consistency is demonstrated with worked matrices (pp.22–25). That checks coherence of a judgment matrix, not empirical truth or representativeness. The app's screen flow is clear (pp.20, 31), but there is no documented user test. Self-improvement through future collected preferences is a proposed capability, not measured performance.

## Static consistency and reproducibility audit

Several inspectable discrepancies affect the connection between the reasoning and the results:

- The basic-customization equation reverses the earlier benefit/penalty signs, and maps an input from 1–9 to alpha=x/5 and beta=1−x/5, permitting negative beta (p.20). Endpoint and monotonicity tests are required before using this interface.
- The printed final formula has seven terms and weights summing to 4.30 (p.27), while the code uses substantially different weights and adds an unweighted length term absent from the formula (pp.33–34). The displayed spreadsheet is consistent in structure with this additional length contribution, so the published ranking must be traced to its actual implementation rather than inferred from the prose alone.
- The survey is described as 60 complete respondents (p.26), but every appendix table reports 62. Several late questions have duplicated or inconsistent labels, complicating reconstruction of the seven-factor comparison matrix (pp.46–49). The published marginal means are not linked by an explicit aggregation procedure to the integer-like matrix entries (p.26).
- The limitations section still says every variable is unweighted despite the weighted final model (p.29). The summary's transformation/imputation order differs from the body. These are model-version integration problems.
- Appendix C retains invalid material labels in the Type column, and its final records contain blank thrill/comprehensive scores despite the earlier statement that all data have been filled (pp.36–38). The cell for Bullet Coaster's normalized duration is negative. These visible output anomalies deserve reconciliation before the archive is used as a reproducible reference.

## Transfer to the working document

Keep the staged construction narrative and the distinction between benefit and discomfort. Require the data role to label measured, imputed and preference-derived inputs separately. Require the method reviewer to challenge latent-construct proxies and transfer of physiological thresholds between contexts. Require the validator to check function endpoints, aggregation bounds, AHP consistency, missing-output counts and rank stability. Freeze the full scoring expression, coefficients and preprocessing order in a run manifest; have the writer generate the summary, final equation and ranking from that manifest. Preserve a separate app personalization layer when the base task asks for specification-only rankings.
