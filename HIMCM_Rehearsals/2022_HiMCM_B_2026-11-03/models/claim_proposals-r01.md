## Suggested summary wording

Using the supplied 1959–2021 annual records, we find that the ten-year CO2 rise ending 2004 was 18.74 ppm, slightly below 18.77 ppm ending 2003. The annual data cannot verify the statement's March-specific assertion. Three continuation models give 2050 concentrations of466.29–496.81 ppm and 2100 concentrations of583.70–688.33 ppm; none reaches the requested 685-ppm pure-CO2 threshold by2050. The OECD source's685 ppm instead denotes greenhouse-gas CO2-equivalent.

Recent drift wins our prespecified pooled-development comparison, but quadratic has lower 2012–2021 CO2 error; no uniformly best future model is established. The selected linked temperature path reaches 1.25, 1.50 and 2.00 C above 1951–1980 in2036, 2048 and 2076, conditional on continuation assumptions. Alternatives and a shorter fitting window move these dates. Level CO2/temperature association weakens after detrending and does not identify causation. Backtests cover at most ten years, and the temperature historical-error envelope misses 2016. Consequently all distant dates are conditional scenarios, while 2031 marks only the maximum tested-horizon display cutoff, not guaranteed reliability.

## Proposed nontechnical article (writer to render within one page)

### Reading the climate numbers carefully

Using the data supplied through 2021, we checked several widely quoted climate numbers. Definitions matter: the supplied CO2 values are annual Mauna Loa averages, despite the question's reference to March. Their ten-year rise ending 2004 was 18.74 parts per million, slightly below the 18.77 rise ending 2003. This does not settle the separate March claim. NOAA's source does support a monthly average of about 421 ppm in May 2022.

We compared three simple ways of extending past CO2 changes. They produce about 466–497 ppm in2050 and 584–688 ppm in2100. None reaches 685 ppm by2050. That is not a refutation of OECD: its cited 685 ppm combines greenhouse gases as CO2-equivalent, a different quantity from CO2 alone.

The choice of model matters. A recent-growth rule performed best in our planned historical development comparison; a quadratic curve performed better in the later 2012–2021 assessment. We therefore cannot name a universally best forecast.

CO2 and global temperature anomalies rise together in these records, but shared trends and other influences prevent this analysis from isolating cause. Our selected linked path reaches 1.25, 1.50 and 2.00 C above the 1951–1980 average in2036, 2048 and 2076. These are conditional annual-path dates, not established deadlines or Paris-target dates. Other models and fitting periods move them.

Tests extend only ten years ahead, and the temperature error reference missed 2016. Extending a curve to2100 is much easier than validating it. Continued measurement, consistent units and comparisons across scenarios are therefore essential. Decisions about interventions should also use evidence on emissions, other climate drivers and response delays; these two records cannot quantify a policy's effect.

## Rounding and presentation contract

Use ppm for concentration, ppm/year for annualized decade growth, degrees C anomaly against 1951–1980 for temperature and integer calendar years for crossings. Tables may use CO2 to two decimals, forecast temperature to two decimals, MAE to3–6 decimals as needed and correlations to3–6 decimals. Those digits reproduce calculations; they do not express future precision. Article ranges may round to whole ppm. Use full stored coefficients/values to draw figures or verify thresholds, never the rounded display formulas. In particular T0's 2088 value rounds to2.00 but is below 2 in raw precision; its crossing remains 2089.

Table selectors must include `fit_window`: never mix full-path values with last 30 dates. The selected last 30 temperature dates 2033/2044/2068 are sensitivity, not replacements. Distinguish log-CO2 correlations from raw-CO2 correlations and full 1959–2021 diagnostics from exploratory through 2011 diagnostics.
