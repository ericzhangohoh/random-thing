SUMMARY SHEET
Carbon dioxide and temperature
A bounded empirical solution to 2022 HiMCM Problem B
Using the supplied annual Mauna Loa CO2 and global temperature records through 2021, we
evaluate historical claims, compare forecast rules and examine how concentration and
temperature move together. The ten-year concentration rise ending in 2004 is 18.74 ppm, slightly
below 18.77 ppm ending in 2003. This small arithmetic difference does not establish physical
significance, and annual observations cannot settle the statement's March-specific claim.
We compare recent drift (D), quadratic growth (Q) and exponential growth (E) using fixed
chronological development forecasts. D minimizes pooled development error, but Q has lower
CO2 error on the later 2012-2021 assessment. Rankings also vary across origins. We retain
the prespecified selection and conclude that no uniformly best future model is established.

None reaches this pure-CO2 threshold by 2050. OECD's cited 685 ppm instead measures
greenhouse gases as CO2-equivalent, so the calculations do not test the same quantity. [6]
The selected sequential temperature path, T1-D, improves on the specified linear-time baseline,
although T1-Q has slightly lower errors. T1-D reaches anomalies of 1.25, 1.50 and 2.00 degrees
C above 1951-1980 in 2036, 2048 and 2076. These are conditional annual central-path
crossings, not realized, sustained or Paris-target dates. Alternative models and fitting windows
change them. Strong level association weakens after detrending and differencing; it does not
identify causation.
Backtests cover at most ten years, and the selected temperature historical-error envelope misses
2016. Thus 2031 is only the corresponding display cutoff, not a validated reliability date. Neither
historical envelopes nor model spread provide probability bounds for distant forecasts.
We recommend consistent measurement definitions, continued monitoring, repeated error
assessment and comparison of alternative assumptions. Estimating intervention effects requires
additional emissions, forcing and response evidence.
